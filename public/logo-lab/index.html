Pick a mark.

Seven angles, one surviving candidate each. Every mark is rendered here against routstr's actual tokens — #111111 background, #161616 card, #2c2c2c border, Geist Mono — not a generic mockup, because the brand is fixed-dark and monochrome with no accent hue to fake. Each candidate sits on both a light and a dark ground at three sizes (64px display, 24px icon, and a favicon-scale mark in the "routstr" lockup) so weaknesses that only show up at small size are visible here too. Those two tiles never move — they're the candidate's own light/dark behavior. The toggle at right only recolors this gallery page's own chrome.

01

Severed Pass

5/5
routstr
routstr

Concept A rounded-square token cut clean through by a single full-height diagonal slit, splitting it into an unequal sliver (~25%) and a main body (~75%). The break is total and irreversible: nothing can rejoin the two pieces, standing for a single-use proof that leaves no reissued record.

Why it fits The cut runs the full height, top edge to bottom edge, so a 15% inset crop still shows it clearly — it can't be sliced away by a badge/safe-area crop the way an edge-corner notch could. Checked at 512/128/64/32/16px: at 16px it's still unmistakably a rounded square split by a bold gap. A shape severed into two pieces that can never be rejoined is a sharp metaphor for "single-use, no reissue."

Weakness Encodes only the Cashu/bearer-token leg of routstr's identity — nothing about Nostr discovery or auto-routing to the cheapest provider. Risks reading generically as "a card with a slash" to a viewer with zero context; it trades a strong-but-wrong association (coupon/voucher) for a weaker, more ambiguous one, and earns specificity more from the routstr story than from the silhouette alone.

One-color test Already true flat #0a0a0a with the cut as genuine cut-through negative space (evenodd), no gradient, no second value — the concept is the silhouette.

02

Single-Pass Gate (v2)

4/5
routstr
routstr

Concept A thick sealed bracket, broken once on its right side, with a single bold bar caught mid-slide through the break — overshooting the boundary as if in transit.

Why it fits Fixes the prior version's cliché match (double-parallel-wall-plus-crossing-line, which collapsed into dagger/turnstile/battery-terminal glyphs) and its occlusion failure: crop the frame away and what's left is an inert capsule with no independent glyph identity, so boundary and bar now depend on each other for meaning.

Weakness Reads as a squared bracket with a plug/tab sliding through a slot — a drawer-eject or socket-and-plug pictogram, still a borrowed mechanical metaphor rather than something derived from what routstr uniquely does. It's also a heavy filled weight class, a real departure from a thin uniform-stroke sibling system if one exists.

One-color test True by construction — one fill/stroke color (#0a0a0a) throughout, no gradient, no second hue, rendered and checked flat at both 512px and 64px; stays legible at 64px where earlier hairline versions of this idea vanished or resolved into an unrelated glyph.

03

Sat Gate

4/5
routstr
routstr

Concept A sealed rounded-square housing with a single directional slot cut fully inside it (never touching the outer boundary): flat-backed on the trailing edge, pointed on the leading edge, sized for exactly one token to pass, one orientation only.

Why it fits A sealed housing with a slot sized for exactly one token, insertable only one way, pointing onward — reads as pay-per-request rather than a persistent open connection. Cropping the 512px render 30% from top+bottom and separately from the left edge leaves the slot fully intact both times, since it never touches the canvas boundary, directly fixing the prior corner-notch version's occlusion failure.

Weakness Says nothing about Nostr discovery — visualizes the payment/metering half only. Remains a category-level metaphor (single-use gated access, vending/turnstile-adjacent) rather than something no other product could plausibly use. The directional point carrying most of the "keyed" meaning is favicon-fragile: below ~48px it's present in outline but doing little rhetorical work.

One-color test Rendered and checked at 512/64/24px in flat #0a0a0a. Holds cleanly at 512 and 64px. At 24px the point softens and it reads mostly as a plain pale rectangle inside a rounded square — degrades gracefully into "plate with a slot," never regressing into a letterform or ambiguous blob.

04

Route Fork

3.5/5
routstr
routstr

Concept A bold geometric "R" (Routstr's initial) whose leg splits into two diverging prongs: one long dominant stroke, one short tapered sliver, with a clean triangular gap of negative space wedged between them where they part ways.

Why it fits The silhouette is no longer a ring and has no gap-in-a-loop reading, so it can't be lifted onto an unrelated "open" brand — it's built from Routstr's own initial letter. The dominant prong is the request going out; the shorter tapered prong is the path not taken this time, present but subordinate, exactly like the un-selected providers in Routstr's auto-routing.

Weakness The fork is the single element carrying the entire "routing" concept — delete it and the mark reverts to a plain, unremarkable capital R. A viewer who doesn't consciously parse "two paths splitting from one stroke" can still read it as nothing more than an R with an odd notch in the leg.

One-color test Single flat #0a0a0a fill, evenodd rule cutting the one true hole (the bowl counter), no gradient or second color. At 512px and 128px reads cleanly as a bold R with a visible forked leg; at true 32px favicon scale it still reads unambiguously as R, with the fork surviving as a smaller but legible notch.

05

Flip Seam

3.5/5
routstr
routstr

Concept A rounded-square silhouette cut by one off-center hairline seam, split ~14:8.5 (close to the golden section) rather than a bisecting 50/50 line.

Why it fits A single rounded-square silhouette cut by one off-center hairline reads as a still frame of a reading that just resettled — the per-request cheapest-provider recompute — without borrowing generic sync/pulse/network iconography. No banned symbol (bolt, gem, node-graph, sync loop) appears anywhere.

Weakness Reduced this far, the mark is a rounded square with a line through it — untethered from LLM-inference/marketplace/pay-per-request semantics without the surrounding brand story. Fails the occlusion test (identity is entirely corner-dependent) and the swap test decisively: the rationale sentence is interchangeable with any "process mid-recompute" product.

One-color test Holds up at 512px — the seam is genuine negative space and reads crisply as one silhouette with one deliberate cut. Does NOT fully hold up at true favicon scale: at 48px the hairline band (<5% of height) is already reduced to a faint sliver and will likely anti-alias into a smear or vanish at real 16–32px favicon size.

Cliché match Not from the explicitly banned list, but lands in a different generic bucket: a rounded-square "card" divided unevenly by a thin bar reads as stock UI-component iconography (a content card with a footer/divider) or, depending on context, a coin-slot/SD-card/cassette-notch silhouette — borrowed app-icon grammar, not a shape invented from the product.

06

Strike

3/5
routstr
routstr

Concept An off-center hairline crease cut as true negative space into a solid rounded square, its diagonal angle deliberately left off-grid — the bid can land anywhere.

Why it fits Built from the token system's own vocabulary — one soft rounded-square radius, one hairline-weight interruption, strict grayscale — and the crease reads as a bid landing arbitrarily rather than a fixed, designed-in badge. Its stroke width was bumped from 1.5 to 1.8 units after rendering showed the thinner version nearly disappearing at small size.

Weakness This is squarely the generic-AI-logo pattern — a decorative geometric interruption in a soft rounded square, a shape family saturated in current minimalist tech branding. Nothing about the mark is derived from what routstr specifically does — no route, no cheapest-price selection, no permissionless discovery, no settlement — it needed a paragraph of designer explanation to mean "bid."

One-color test This claim holds up: the crease is a true mask cutout, not a stroke needing a second color, so it renders as a clean, legible cutout with no gradient dependency and would invert cleanly to the opposite ground. The strongest part of the execution.

Cliché match A single diagonal stroke inside a soft rounded square is one of the most saturated tropes in current SaaS/AI-startup identity work (the "rounded-square-plus-one-geometric-interruption" pattern across dozens of 2023–2026 fintech/AI rebrands). Also reads instantly as a generic UI glyph family — mute/off slash, minus icon, swipe affordance — before it reads as anything routstr-specific.

07

Low Channel

3/5
routstr
routstr

Concept A single hairline groove cut into a rounded square, sagging low and off-center toward one corner like a shallow trough at rest — not a straight cut, not a symmetric dip.

Why it fits A single continuous line that visibly settles toward whichever corner is lower — never centered, never straight — is a literal visual translation of "the request drains to whatever provider is cheapest right now," with no hub-spoke, arrow, or bolt required to say it. Rendered and viewed at both 512px and 64px, the single-path construction holds up as flat one-color silhouette with no gradient crutch.

Weakness Stripped of the routing story, this is a generic sine-wave/swoosh mark — a shape family already heavily used for "flow," "audio," or "motion" branding. Cropping the outer ~30% removes both terminal anchor points, leaving only an unremarkable S-shaped stroke that reads as a fragment of any generic wave/swoosh icon; the sentence "settles toward whichever corner is lower" ports cleanly to a water utility or a sleep app.

One-color test Holds up: the mark is a single evenodd path (square minus a tapered sliver), so it renders as a clean, legible cutout with no gradient dependency, and would invert cleanly to the opposite ground. The construction claim is honest — the failure is conceptual/associative, not colorimetric.

Cliché match Sine-wave / swoosh / audio-waveform mark — one of the most heavily used shape families in tech branding: signal icons, "flow"/"trend" branding, audio-visualizer logos, and Nike-swoosh-adjacent sport marks all live in exactly this territory.
